# White Blazes: Scene-by-Scene Outline

## How to read this outline

This outline breaks the story in *A Walk in the Woods Game: Story & Endings* (docs/story.md) into **16 scenes**: an opening, 8 decision events, 2 quiet story beats, a final choice and 4 endings. It keeps the same story, characters and stats.

Each scene lists:
- **Purpose:** why the scene exists.
- **What happens:** the events, and how the characters develop.
- **Choices → consequences:** the options and their stat changes.
- **Arcs & themes:** what the scene adds to the bigger story.

**Stats:** Stamina, Supplies, Bond and Morale run 0–10, and Miles is a counter. They start at Stamina 6, Supplies 6, Bond 5, Morale 6.
**Ending checks:** after Event 5, after Event 8, and at the Final Crossroads.

**Scope target:** each scene is about one screen of narration and dialogue plus one choice menu. The whole game plays in about 20–30 minutes.

## Act I: The Beginning (Georgia, early spring)

**Act goal:** set up the big, naive plan, introduce Katz, and teach the player that every choice has a cost.

### Scene 1: Opening (the kitchen table and the airport)
- **Purpose:** establish the premise, the narrator's voice, and the gap between plan and reality.
- **What happens:** the Hiker, buried in maps, announces a 2,190-mile hike. Every invitation is declined except Katz's, which comes too quickly. Katz arrives in Atlanta wheeling a suitcase.
- **Character development:** the Hiker is confident, organized and slightly smug. Katz is a comic disaster with hidden eagerness.
- **Choices:** none. The player names their character here.
- **Arcs & themes:** *expectations vs. reality* begins, and the narrator's voice is at its most planned and superior.

### Scene 2: Event 1, The Outfitter (tutorial)
- **Purpose:** teach the choice menu and the stats in a low-stakes, funny setting. Introduces Dale.
- **What happens:** a pushy salesman and Dale the Gear Guy argue about what the pack needs.
- **Character development:** shows whether the Hiker trusts experts, minimalism, or their friend.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Buy everything: Supplies +2, Stamina −1.
  - Pack ultralight like Dale: Stamina +1, Supplies −1.
  - Let Katz choose: Morale +1, Bond +1, Supplies −1. Sets up the snack cakes.
- **Arcs & themes:** *preparation* is the Hiker's belief system, and this is the first test of it.

### Scene 3: Event 2, "I'm Going Home" (day two)
- **Purpose:** the first real conflict, and the first relationship choice.
- **What happens:** on a muddy climb, Katz sits down and quits. Sunshine has just jogged past, which makes it worse.
- **Character development:** reveals how the Hiker treats Katz under pressure: as a goal-holder, a friend, or a joker.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Push him: Miles +, Bond −1. He follows in silence.
  - Sit with him: Bond +2, Supplies −1, slower pace.
  - Make a joke: Morale +1. He laughs and stands up.
- **Arcs & themes:** starts *Katz's arc* and the *relationship* arc, and introduces humor as a way of coping.

### Scene 4: Act I close (the first night)
- **Purpose:** a quiet beat of wonder after the struggle, and the first crack in the Hiker's armor.
- **What happens:** at a crowded shelter, the Hiker sees a sky full of stars while Katz snores. *"Am I hiking the trail, or is it hiking me?"*
- **Choices:** none. The narration changes slightly depending on Bond.
- **Arcs & themes:** *the beauty and harshness of the wilderness*, and the narrator's voice starts to soften.

## Act II: The Middle (Smokies and Virginia)

**Act goal:** raise the pressure, test the partnership, and tempt the player to bend the original plan.

### Scene 5: Event 3, Whiteout (Smokies)
- **Purpose:** the first danger from nature, forcing a choice between safety, toughness and flexibility.
- **What happens:** a spring snowstorm hits. The shelter is packed, and Dale is lecturing about socks.
- **Character development:** the Hiker's "stick to the plan" mindset meets weather that doesn't care about plans.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Wait in the shelter: Stamina +1, Morale −1.
  - Push on: Miles +, Stamina −2.
  - Side trail to town: Supplies +1, Miles −. This is the first hint that the plan can bend.
- **Arcs & themes:** *adversity* from nature, and an early seed of the *Changed Route* idea.

### Scene 6: Event 4, Night Visitor (Smokies)
- **Purpose:** comic relief after the storm, and a small trust test.
- **What happens:** something snuffles outside the tent. Katz is sure it's a bear and narrates his own obituary. It turns out to be a raccoon in every branch, so the payoff is always the same.
- **Character development:** Katz's fear is played for laughs, and the player decides whether to go along with his plan.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Hang the food bag properly: Stamina −1.
  - Katz's plan, banging pots: Bond +1, Morale +1, Supplies −1 (the raccoon takes a bagel).
  - Ignore it: Supplies −2.
- **Arcs & themes:** *humor as coping*, and the raccoon becomes a running joke used again later.

### Scene 7: Event 5, Pine Gap (Virginia)
- **Purpose:** the biggest temptation in the game, and the first ending check.
- **What happens:** at Hazel's diner, the hot, monotonous Virginia stretch has worn everyone down. A truck is heading 100 miles north, and Katz wants to ride. Hazel delivers the game's key line about "the quitting you don't do."
- **Character development:** Katz shows his shortcut-seeking side. The Hiker's integrity, or their flexibility, is tested.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Zero day: Stamina +2, Morale +1, Miles −.
  - Resupply and go: Supplies +2, Morale −1.
  - Ride ahead together: Miles + (big), Morale +1, and **sets the "skipped ahead" flag**, which rules out Finished Trail.
  - Let Katz ride alone: Bond −2.
- **Ending check:** Bond 2 or less leads to Broken Partnership. Stamina or Supplies at 0, or Morale 2 or less, leads to Early Departure.
- **Arcs & themes:** *expectations vs. reality* comes to a head. What counts as "really" hiking the trail?

### Scene 8: Event 6, The Last Bottle (Virginia)
- **Purpose:** a hard choice between survival and friendship.
- **What happens:** in a heatwave the spring on the map is dry, there's one liter left, and Katz is fading.
- **Character development:** the Hiker chooses between rules and generosity, and Katz notices which one it is.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Split it evenly: Bond +2, Stamina −1.
  - Ration by plan: Supplies held, Bond −1.
  - Gamble on a spring off-trail: a coin flip for Morale +2, or Stamina −1 and Miles −.
- **Arcs & themes:** *adversity* and the *relationship*. This is the clearest example of a choice that costs one thing to gain another.

### Scene 9: Midpoint, the campfire talk
- **Purpose:** the emotional center of the game. It shows the player what their choices have built so far.
- **What happens:** at a campfire, the conversation changes with Bond:
  - **Bond 5+:** Katz admits his life had stalled and this trip was the first thing he'd committed to in years. The Hiker answers honestly about why they came.
  - **Bond 4 or less:** Katz says he's been checking bus schedules.
  - In both versions, Sunshine passes and says: "Nobody walks the trail they planned. You walk the one that shows up."
- **Choices:** none. It's a reflection scene.
- **Arcs & themes:** the turning point for *personal growth* and *Katz's arc*. From here the narrator's voice is noticeably more reflective.

## Act III: The Climax (Maine, autumn)

**Act goal:** the hardest, most beautiful setting. Every earlier choice now pays off or comes due.

### Scene 10: Event 7, The River (Hundred-Mile Wilderness)
- **Purpose:** a physical test that rewards a strong partnership and punishes earlier exhaustion.
- **What happens:** a rain-swollen river blocks the trail, and there's no road or town for days.
- **Character development:** locking arms to ford it is a literal act of trust. How Katz reacts shows how far he has come.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Ford it together: Bond +1, Stamina −1. If Stamina is 3 or less, you also lose Supplies −1 to the current.
  - Wait for the water to drop: Supplies −2, Morale −1.
  - Detour upstream: Stamina −1, Miles −.
- **Arcs & themes:** *adversity*, plus the *relationship* shown through action rather than words.

### Scene 11: Event 8, The Fall
- **Purpose:** the game's biggest sacrifice choice, and the second ending check.
- **What happens:** Katz twists his ankle on wet roots and insists he's fine. He isn't.
- **Character development:** the clearest test of what the Hiker values: the goal, the friend, or safety. If Bond is high, Katz's thank-you line lands as the payoff of his arc.
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - Carry his pack: Stamina −2, Bond +2.
  - Go ahead alone for help: Miles +, Bond −1.
  - Walk him out to a logging road: Morale −1, and **sets the "left the wilderness" flag**. The Final Crossroads then happens at a roadside campground, and "Summit or nothing" leads to Changed Route.
- **Ending check:** the same rules as Scene 7.
- **Arcs & themes:** the climax of *Katz's arc* and of the *personal growth* arc.

### Scene 12: The Final Crossroads
- **Purpose:** the player states, in their own choice, what success means to them.
- **What happens:** at the last campfire the final mountain is on the horizon. The narration sums up the journey (tired bodies, how much food is left, how much trust is left), changing with the stats. Katz asks, "So what now?"
- **Choices → consequences:**
  - "Summit or nothing": Finished Trail if Stamina 5+, Supplies 3+, Bond 5+ and no flags are set. Otherwise Changed Route.
  - "Let's go home": Early Departure.
  - "Let's finish it our way": Changed Route.
- **Arcs & themes:** resolves *expectations vs. reality*. The player decides whether the original goal still matters.

## The Four Endings

The game checks these in order, and the first that fits wins. Each ending is one short scene followed by an ending card showing miles walked, a Bond rating (Strangers, Hiking Buddies or Trail Family) and a final Katz line.

### Scene 13: Broken Partnership
- **Triggered by:** Bond 2 or less at any check.
- **Purpose:** show that ignoring the relationship has a cost greater than miles.
- **What happens:** Katz buys a bus ticket at the next road. The Hiker walks alone for a day, finds the silence unbearable, and goes home. Weeks later a postcard arrives showing a raccoon holding a bagel.
- **Character resolution:** the Hiker realizes too late what Katz meant to the trip. The postcard leaves room for repair.
- **Theme:** *no goal is worth more than the people you take with you.*

### Scene 14: Early Departure
- **Triggered by:** Stamina or Supplies at 0, Morale 2 or less at a check, or choosing "Let's go home."
- **Purpose:** show that stopping can be honest rather than shameful.
- **What happens:** a motel near a trailhead, a map spread on the bed. The Hiker looks at the miles left, and Katz points at the miles behind: *"We walked from there to here. On purpose."*
- **Character resolution:** the Hiker lets go of perfectionism. If Bond is high, Katz is the one comforting *them*.
- **Theme:** *adversity sometimes wins, and that is not the same as failing.*

### Scene 15: Finished Trail
- **Triggered by:** "Summit or nothing" with Stamina 5+, Supplies 3+, Bond 5+, and neither the skipped-ahead nor the left-the-wilderness flag set.
- **Purpose:** reward careful, balanced play: preparation *and* friendship.
- **What happens:** the summit at sunrise above a sea of clouds. Katz complains all the way up, then goes quiet. One photo. Sunshine is already there, cheering.
- **Character resolution:** the Hiker's goal is achieved, but the narration focuses on Katz and the view, not the mileage.
- **Theme:** *preparation, care for each other and stubbornness can carry you through.*

### Scene 16: Changed Route (the "true" ending)
- **Triggered by:** "Let's finish it our way", trying for the summit without the stats, or either flag being set.
- **Purpose:** the ending closest to the book's spirit. Success is redefined, not abandoned.
- **What happens:** the pair choose the stretches that meant something, walk them at their own pace, and end on a quiet lakeshore. *"We didn't hike the whole thing. We hiked our thing."*
- **Character resolution:** both characters accept the trail on its own terms. This is the full version of the narrator's softened voice.
- **Theme:** *growth comes from changing your goals when reality changes.*

## Continuity Rules and Callbacks

These rules keep the story consistent whichever path the player takes.

**Running threads**
- **Snack cakes:** bought in Event 1 (if Katz chose), shared in Event 2. If Katz didn't choose the gear, he packed a few "for emergencies" anyway, so the Event 2 option always makes sense.
- **The raccoon:** always revealed in Event 4, whichever choice you make, and it returns on the Broken Partnership postcard.
- **Dale:** appears in Event 1 and Event 3, and makes a one-line cameo at the river in Event 7 ("Should've packed a packraft").
- **Sunshine:** passes you in Act I, gives her advice at the midpoint, and is waiting at the summit in Finished Trail. She is the measuring stick the Hiker stops comparing themselves to.
- **Hazel's line:** "the quitting you don't do" is said in Event 5 and echoed by the narrator in Early Departure or Finished Trail.

**Flags (yes/no story facts)**
- **Skipped ahead** (Event 5): blocks Finished Trail, and Katz jokes about it at the Final Crossroads.
- **Left the wilderness** (Event 8): moves the Final Crossroads to a roadside campground and blocks Finished Trail.

**The narrator's voice by act**
- **Act I:** plans, gear lists and mileage math.
- **Act II:** starts noticing the scenery, the people and Katz.
- **Act III and endings:** reflective and honest. The same narrator, now changed. This is the clearest way the player experiences personal growth.

**Katz's tone follows Bond:** at 7+ he's warmer and the jokes are affectionate; at 3–4 they're sharper and more sarcastic. Only the line choice changes, so this needs no extra scenes.

## Keeping It Under 10 Hours

The story is built so it can be produced quickly:

- **One template for every event:** narration, two or three lines of dialogue, a choice menu, and a one-line result showing the stat changes.
- **Endings are illustrated text cards**, not animated cutscenes.
- **Side characters are cameos:** each has three lines or fewer per scene.
- **Bond-based variation is limited** to the midpoint and Katz's final line, so there are no separate branches to write.

**If time runs short, cut in this order (the story still holds together):**
1. Dale's cameo at the river (Event 7).
2. The coin-flip option in Event 6. Keep the two fixed choices.
3. The Bond-based narration variants in the Act I close and the Final Crossroads. Use one version.
4. Merge Events 3 and 4 into a single Smokies event (keep Whiteout) and move the raccoon into its narration.

**Never cut:** Event 2, Event 5, Event 8, the midpoint, the Final Crossroads, or any of the four endings. They carry the main arcs and the essay themes.
