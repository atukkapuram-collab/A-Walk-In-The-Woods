Game Planning Overview

Interactive “A Walk in the Woods” Based Game → Decision based and Oregon Trail inspired
Project Overview
Project Goal
	Create a narrative and strategy game based on Bill Bryson’s novel “A Walk in the Woods”. The game is inspired by the playstyle and narrative style of the Oregon Trail game, and will follow a similar art direction of games like Stardew Valley and Terraria. The game should serve as a good alternative to writing an essay by allowing us, the developers, to apply our knowledge from reading this book and class lectures/teachings; as well, it will allow the player to experience similar character development and difficult circumstances that Bill Bryson faced, letting the player immerse themselves in the actual trail.
Game Concept:
A 2d pixel art adventure narrative style game that is based off of Bill Bryson’s journey through the Appalachian trail in “A Walk in the Woods”. The goal is to have the player travel across the Appalachian Trail similar to the Oregon Trail and Bill Bryson’s journey. Throughout the game the player will face challenges of hiking in the wild, by making decisions that affect the overall hiking experience.

Game Development Directions
Design Direction
Visuals:
	2d pixel art game with a top-angled perspective similar to the older pokemon game style of exploration.
Artistic Style and Requirements:
	The game should combine both the beauty and visual appeal of the trail’s environment and the demanding, harsh reality of the trail’s wilderness.
	The game should include basic:
Forests, Mountains, Rivers, Rocky Paths, Campsites
Character Sprites with Simple Animations
Minimum three to four settings (each setting explored top-down in the older Pokemon style), including landscapes and isolated portions of the trail
Weather and lighting to reflect the changing conditions
Slightly rugged and adventurous aesthetic, yet still presented slightly cute or cozy
	The game will draw inspiration of the visuals from games like Stardew Valley and Terraria, while also being combined with the small, contained exploration of a wilderness adventure game.
Gameplay Direction
	The game will follow a simple and straightforward structure:
Explore the area
Encounter a challenge
Make a decision
Experience the decisions consequence
Continue progressing the trail
Repeat
`	The game will have the player explore various sections of the Appalachian Trail in order to get a quick overview of the whole trail, while facing interactive obstacles which get resolved through decisions.
	The game should include:
Exploration of minimum three to four settings (each setting explored top-down in the older Pokemon style), including landscapes and isolated portions of the trail
Decisions from hiking challenges and unexpected obstacles
Management of a few basic resources
Decisions affecting progress and relationships with the companion
Interactions with companion and other character’s
Three to four possible endings depending on the decisions the player makes along their journey
Narrative Direction
	The game will have a simple overarching story of an ordinary character and his friend trying to best the Appalachian Trail. It will not be a direct recreation of the book, rather it will include characters and settings similar to the book.
	Similar to the book, the player will go on a journey with expectations to complete the whole trail; however, as the player progresses through the trail, they will face situations that require difficult decisions that will affect whether or not they complete their journey.
	The narrative will emphasize the following themes in order to ensure that us as developers still understand english teachings:
Adversity → Includes the essay question into the game
Personal Growth
	The game will utilize narrative dialogue, character dialogue, and humor to reflect a similar style that Bill Bryson used when telling his story.
Possible Endings
	The game will have at least three to four endings based on the decisions that the player makes along their journey. A few of the original endings will include:
Finished Trail
The player and their companion reach the destination by overcoming all the challenges and obstacles of the trail.
	Determining Factors: Trail Progress, Resource Management, Good-Enough Physical Condition
Broken Partnership
The player and his companion have encounter too many disagreements conflicts that lead to a relationship so broken that they both decide to return home.
Determining Factors: Poor Companion Relationship, Poor Cooperation Decisions, Personal Choices Over Partnership
Early Departure
	The player and their companion leave the trail early because the trails challenge become overwhelming.
	Determining Factors: Poor Physical Condition, Lack of Resources, Poor Preparation for Obstacles and Difficult Circumstances
Changed Route
	The player and their companion face an ending most similar to Bill Bryon’s journey. In this ending the two will abandon their original goal to make it to the end of the trial, and rather make the most of their experience.
	Determining Factors: Decisions during Obstacles, Remaining Resources, Willingness and Remaining Motivation
